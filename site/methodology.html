Methodology

Background: Williams's commitment problem, in brief

Williams (§2.4) separates commitment into three aspects: intentions are conduct-controlling (they cause the action), they settle among alternatives so that an agent holds a mutually consistent set of them, and they have stability: after settling, one normally stops deliberating and becomes resistant to reconsideration, though not immune to it. Intentions also frame planning. Against these he finds that the best current candidate representations inside an LLM represent alternative, incompatible outcomes side by side, that cross-token maintenance can break down, and that a model playing hangman can reveal one word midway and accept another at the end. His unit of analysis is a representation inside the model. This study asks what happens when the unit of analysis is instead the complete coding agent: model, harness, conversation and repository, observed over many inference and tool steps.
